Laboratory Investigation

Experimental Investigation of Wind Turbine Performance

Energy Balance, Efficiency, and Wake Behaviour of HAWT and Savonius VAWT Rotors

Institution London South Bank University
Academic Level Year 2 — BEng Mechanical Engineering
Testing Rig Armfield RE12 Wind Energy System
Key Conditions T = 23.0°C | ρ = 1.1731 kg/m³ | R = 33 Ω

1. Project Overview & Objectives

This controlled laboratory investigation evaluated how small-scale horizontal-axis and vertical-axis wind turbine rotors convert incoming aerodynamic power into electrical power[cite: 5, 16]. Using the Armfield RE12 Advanced Wind Energy System, measurements of upstream and downstream air velocity, generator voltage, and load current were recorded across 8 variable fan supply settings (5 V to 12 V)[cite: 5, 16].

  • Theoretical Benchmarking: Applied fluid mechanics and the Betz limit (59.3% theoretical maximum extraction) to quantify practical losses[cite: 5, 16].
  • Aerodynamic Comparison: Examined performance differences between lift-based (HAWT) and drag-based (Savonius VAWT) aerodynamic operating regimes[cite: 5, 16].
  • Wake Analysis: Analyzed downstream airflow velocity to determine energy extraction and wake deceleration across the swept area[cite: 5, 16].

2. Experimental Apparatus & Rotor Geometry

HAWT (Lift-Based) 3 Blades | 25° Pitch | Radius: 62 mm | Swept Area: 0.012076 m²
Savonius VAWT (Drag-Based) Vertical Axis | Ø 80.3 mm × 60 mm H | Projected Area: 0.004818 m²

The testing apparatus comprised an axial flow fan with a flow-straightening duct, variable DC power supply, generator circuit with a fixed 33 Ω resistive load, and digital anemometer calibrated for upstream and downstream boundary readings[cite: 5, 17].

3. Governing Engineering Equations

Calculations were carried out systematically using standard thermofluid dynamics formulations[cite: 17, 18]:

Available Wind Power: Pw = 0.5 × ρ × A × Vw³
Electrical Power Output: Pr = U × I
Turbine Power Coefficient: Cp = Pr / Pw
Efficiency Relative to Betz Limit: η = (Pr / (0.593 × Pw)) × 100%

4. Experimental Findings & Data Summary

The two rotor topologies demonstrated distinctly different behaviour under identical airflow conditions[cite: 5, 18]:

Parameter (@ 6.00 m/s Upstream Speed) HAWT (3-Blade) Savonius VAWT
Peak Electrical Output 234.00 mW 13.11 mW
Efficiency Relative to Betz Limit 25.79% 3.62%
Starting Torque & Low-Speed Behavior Cut-in threshold required > 3.6 m/s Self-started reliably at lowest speed (2.5 m/s)

Engineering Insight: While the lift-based HAWT achieved dramatically higher conversion efficiency at higher air velocities, the Savonius rotor demonstrated superior starting torque at low wind speeds, illustrating trade-offs in urban or micro-scale wind generation[cite: 5, 18].

5. Key Competencies Demonstrated

  • Thermofluids & aerodynamic testing using calibrated instrumentation (anemometers, shunt resistors, variable power modules)[cite: 5, 19].
  • Data processing, error quantification, Betz-limit benchmarking, and power curve generation[cite: 5, 19].
  • Technical communication: translating raw laboratory readings into structured engineering documentation for stakeholders[cite: 5, 19].
