Rotor Topologies & Geometry

HAWT (Lift-Based) 3 Blades | 25° Pitch | Radius: 62 mm | Swept Area: 0.012076 m²
Savonius VAWT (Drag-Based) Vertical Axis | Ø 80.3 mm × 60 mm H | Projected Area: 0.004818 m²

The HAWT provides an area ratio 2.506× larger than the Savonius rotor, directly scaling the available incoming kinetic wind power across identical upstream velocities.

Method & Governing Calculations

  1. Established ambient laboratory baselines (temperature, air density, humidity) and verified physical rotor dimensions.
  2. Calibrated baseline upstream wind velocity across 8 excitation settings (5.0 V to 12.0 V) without rotors fitted.
  3. Installed the three-blade HAWT; logged upstream velocity, downstream wake velocity, voltage, and current across all fan voltages.
  4. Fitted the Savonius VAWT and recorded identical aerodynamic and electrical parameters.
  5. Processed raw readings to evaluate available power, output power, Betz limits, and irreversible loss mechanisms.
Available Wind Power: Pw = 0.5 × ρ × A × Vw³
Electrical Power Output: Pr = U × I
Power Extraction Coefficient: Cp = Pr / Pw
Betz-Limit Reference Power: P_Betz = 0.593 × Pw
Efficiency Relative to Betz: η = (Pr / P_Betz) × 100%

Systematic Rotor Trade-Off Matrix

Engineering Criterion HAWT (3-Blade Lift Rotor) Savonius VAWT (Drag Rotor)
1. Axis & Orientation Horizontal axis; requires active yaw alignment to follow wind vector. Vertical axis; omnidirectional (360°) capture without yaw mechanisms.
2. Rotational Speed (TSR) High shaft speed; tip-speed ratio λ > 1 enables efficient generator coupling. Low shaft speed; bucket cannot exceed incoming wind velocity (λ < 1).
3. Starting Dynamics Stalled below 3.60 m/s due to blade stall and bearing stiction. Self-starting down to 2.51 m/s via positive static cup drag.
4. Terminal Power Output High peak conversion: 234.00 mW output (26.91% peak Betz efficiency). Limited peak output: 13.11 mW (max 3.62% Betz efficiency).
5. Operating Principle Bernoulli pressure differentials generate driving aerodynamic lift. Differential drag force between advancing concave and returning convex cups.

Thermodynamic Losses & Exergy Dissipation

  • Wake Kinetic Deficit: Betz theorem proves an ideal disk exit velocity of Vw/3 (33.3%). The HAWT wake exited at 3.80 m/s (63.3% of upstream), dissipating kinetic exergy into downstream swirl.
  • Tip Vortex & Flow Bypass: Without tip winglets, high-pressure air spills over the 62 mm blade tips into spanwise tip vortices, causing aerodynamic induced drag.
  • Electromechanical Dissipation: Available work is irreversibly degraded (ΔS_gen > 0) through mechanical bearing friction and electrical generator I²R resistive heat dissipation.
