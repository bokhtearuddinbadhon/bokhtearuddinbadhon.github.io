1. The Mechanical Engineering Question

In structural mechanics, supports control how forces and moments are transmitted while allowing or restricting degrees of freedom (DoF). A slider support occupies the fundamental boundary between a pin support (which resists translation but allows rotation) and a fixed support (which prevents all motion and rotation).

Statics Equilibrium & Degrees of Freedom:
Under ideal frictionless mechanics: $\sum F_y = 0$, $\sum M_z = 0$, while $\sum F_x = 0$ remains unrestrained along the guide axis. The guide assembly provides reaction forces against vertical loads and moments while permitting pure horizontal travel.

2. Mechanism Architecture & CAD Design

Modeled from initial technical sketches into 3D CAD parametric components:

  • Base & Linear Guide Rod: A 70 mm longitudinal base fitted with a precision 6 mm diameter guide rod establishing the translation datum.
  • Sliding Collar: Cylindrical sliding sleeve with engineered bore clearance to balance smooth sliding against excessive angular play.
  • Rigid L-Bracket Loading Arm: Fixed cantilever member enabling progressive dead-weight vertical hanger testing.

3. Support Boundary Condition Comparison

Support Condition Horizontal Translation (X) Vertical Translation (Y) In-Plane Rotation (θ) Reaction Produced
Pin / Hinge Support Restrained Restrained Free $R_x$, $R_y$
Fixed / Built-In Support Restrained Restrained Restrained $R_x$, $R_y$, $M_z$
Slider Support (This Model) Permitted (Free) Restrained Restrained $R_y$, $M_z$

4. Experimental Evaluation & Physical Observations

  1. Controlled Vertical Loading: Incremental slotted masses were applied to the cantilever hanger arm.
  2. Restraint Verification: Zero measurable vertical deflection or tilt rotation was observed along the collar body under design load.
  3. Unrestrained Motion: The assembly maintained free horizontal translation across the guide axis throughout loading.
  4. Real Friction Factors: Surface layer stepping inherent to FDM PLA printing introduced minor sliding friction ($\mu > 0$), distinguishing physical mechanics from theoretical frictionless equilibrium.
